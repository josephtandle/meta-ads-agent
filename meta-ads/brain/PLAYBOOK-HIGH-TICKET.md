# High-Ticket Playbook

## How to use this file

Use this for trust-heavy education, advisory, consulting, implementation, and mastermind-style offers. Read `OFFER-CONTEXT.md` and the account strategy before selecting a funnel. These are operating ranges, not promised outcomes. Every script and landing page must pass the policy pre-flight before it is submitted.

## Select the funnel by price, trust, and sales motion

Funnel selection follows the amount of trust and explanation required, not an aesthetic preference for webinars or calls.

| Price band | Starting funnel | Why it fits | Primary event |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sub-$1K | VSL or webinar to direct checkout | Buying friction can be handled without a sales conversation | Purchase, or a truthful shallower event if volume is thin |
| $1K to $4K | Webinar funnel, often with application and call | The buyer needs education, proof, and an informed next step | Qualified application, then booked call and show |
| $5K+ | VSL to application to call | The sale is trust-heavy and normally needs qualification and sales capacity | Qualified application, booked call, attended call, closed-won |

The hybrid for mid-ticket and complex offers is **VSL -> webinar -> application -> call**. The VSL earns attention and establishes the problem, mechanism, and relevance. The webinar creates the depth, proof, objection handling, and shared vocabulary required for a serious application. The application filters fit and gives sales context; the call closes a qualified opportunity. Skipping the VSL normally harms webinar registration because the prospect has not yet accepted the relevance of the long-form event. Skipping the webinar normally harms close rate because the application call must now do education, objection handling, and the sale at once [practitioner-consensus].

Application rate of about 5% of landing-page visitors can be viable and 15%+ can be excellent; visitor-to-sale often sits around 0.3 to 2% [practitioner-consensus]. These are diagnostic reference points, not targets to promise. A challenge funnel can work where participation itself delivers a meaningful experience, but reported 70 to 80% participant conversion is **[single-source]** and too optimistic to use as a forecast.

## The full KPI chain and what it protects

Never judge this category on CPL alone. The full chain is:

`CPL -> CPQL -> cost per booked call -> cost per show -> CAC -> LTV:CAC`

CPQL means cost per qualified application or lead. It is the first point where the campaign can distinguish curiosity from commercial fit. Cost per booked call is useful for capacity planning but is **meaningless alone**: it can look excellent while sales receives unqualified, unresponsive, or unreachable people. Cost per show exposes calendar and reminder performance. CAC connects spend to closed revenue. LTV:CAC forces the acquisition decision to survive refunds, delivery cost, and future value.

Cost per booked call commonly falls in a $60 to $220 reference range [practitioner-consensus]. It is still meaningless alone. A $60 booked call that produces no shows or closes can be worse than a $220 booked call that yields a profitable, retained customer. Use the number to find a stage change, then trace the entire chain before changing media.

Use CAPI and offline conversions to pass qualified application, booked call, attended call, and closed-won back to Meta. Optimise to the deepest event that can plausibly generate about 50 weekly conversions [meta-official]. If closed-won volume cannot support learning, use qualified applications or booked calls, while retaining deeper events as quality signals. Reports of 15 to 25% CPA improvement after four to six weeks of deeper CAPI signals are directional practitioner claims [practitioner-consensus], not a guarantee.

## Qualification is a deliberate trade-off

Qualifying questions on a lead form or application usually raise CPL 10 to 15% and reduce raw volume 30 to 40%, while improving CPQL [practitioner-consensus]. That is often the right exchange. Ask only questions that a sales team will use: problem context, implementation readiness, authority, timing, or a relevant operational constraint. Do not collect sensitive personal information merely to enrich targeting.

The test is not “did CPL rise?” The test is whether cost per qualified application, show rate, close rate, and CAC improve enough to justify less volume. Send the qualified/not-qualified outcome back through CAPI so the platform does not keep selecting for low-friction form completion.

## Show rate is the underestimated growth lever

Show rate often beats media buying as the fastest way to create more selling conversations. At 100 booked calls, a 50% show rate creates 50 conversations. At 80%, it creates 80. That is a 60% increase in conversations at the same spend: `(80 - 50) / 50 = 60%` [single-source].

Instrument booked time, reminders sent, confirmation, reschedule, attended, qualified, and closed-won. Improve show rate with immediate confirmation, calendar choice, explicit expectation setting, concise reminders, a low-friction reschedule path, and fast follow-up to no-shows. Do not use coercive or misleading reminders.

A useful stage-cascade reference is 40% attend, 50% book, 50% close [single-source]. It illustrates compounding leakage, not an expected benchmark. A campaign can have a respectable booked-call cost and still fail because the booking-to-show or show-to-close stage is broken.

## Budget floors and a worked example

The learning budget formula is `target CPA x 50 / 7` [meta-official]. A practical starting range is often $50 to $250 per day per ad set [practitioner-consensus], but the formula has priority. Underfunding a deep event keeps it in learning, burns budget, and supplies too little feedback.

Worked example: assume the current meaningful optimisation event is a qualified application with a target CPA of $140. The learning floor is `$140 x 50 / 7 = $1,000/day`. If $1,000/day is not fundable, do not set an artificial $100/day budget and expect qualified-application learning. Optimise to a shallower but still truthful event, such as a completed application, then pass qualified application, booked call, show, and closed-won as CAPI signals. Graduate deeper only when volume supports it.

Use `plan-budget` to calculate the account-specific floor and allocation, and use `audit-account` to check whether delivery and measurement support the chosen optimisation event.

For economics, use an LTV:CAC floor of at least 3:1 as a common reference [practitioner-consensus]. A first-pass **CAC ceiling is 30% of offer price** when real close, margin, and refund data are incomplete [practitioner-consensus]. Replace that shortcut with actual unit economics as soon as data exists. Increase a stable winner 15 to 20% every 3 to 4 days, not in abrupt jumps [practitioner-consensus].

## Creative and targeting

High-ticket creative transfers trust before it asks for an application. Founder-face long-form authority, often 40 to 60 seconds or more, can carry a first-person story, problem framing, mechanism, permissible proof, and clear next step [practitioner-consensus]. Narrative usually lasts longer than discount-led creative because it gives a prospect a way to understand the offer. Proof must be cleared and accurately framed, such as “clients have reported...” rather than a universal outcome claim.

Broad and Advantage+ prospecting are the default. Interest stacking has been weakened by automation and interest consolidation [practitioner-consensus]. Use high-value lookalikes based on booked-call or closed-won outcomes when data supports them, not raw lead lookalikes. Retarget by recency: the freshest roughly two weeks can receive a direct application or booking CTA; older visitors need education and proof before another ask.

## POLICY PRE-FLIGHT

Run this literal checklist against every ad, form, landing page, and destination chain. A failed item means do not submit.

1. **Personal attributes:** Does any wording imply the viewer's financial, health, relationship, employment, or other sensitive status? Remove it. “You are broke” style framing is a high-risk personal-attributes violation.
2. **Income claims:** Does the ad promise, imply, or anchor a dollar income outcome? Remove it. Explain skills, systems, community, or process instead. Guaranteed-result wording is also high risk.
3. **One-LP rule:** Does each ad lead to one clear landing page whose content, offer, and next step match what the reviewer sees in the ad? No bait-and-switch chains or mismatched destinations.
4. **Clean infra:** Is the domain, account, pixel, device history, and business infrastructure clean and authorised? Do not reuse restricted infrastructure or attempt to obscure identity.
5. **No engagement bait:** Remove comment-to-unlock, like-to-enter, or engagement-for-access mechanics. They are standing violations, not a clever funnel trick.
6. **Circumventing-systems near-zero recovery:** Do not cloak, use character camouflage, recycle banned IP/device/domain infrastructure, or create deceptive page chains. “Circumventing Systems” enforcement has near-zero recovery [practitioner-consensus]. A new account on contaminated shared infrastructure can be flagged immediately.
7. **Special Ad Category check:** Coaching, consulting, and education offers adjacent to employment, credit, or financial opportunity can be auto-classified into a Special Ad Category, which silently strips targeting and lookalike options. Check the campaign's category assignment before judging delivery, and never try to relabel an offer to dodge the classification. That is a circumventing-systems flag.

Repeat income-claim and personal-attribute rejections can accumulate account risk [practitioner-consensus]. Policy compliance is part of creative quality, not a final legal pass.

## What to test without breaking the sales motion

Prioritise tests that improve one stage of the chain while preserving the rest: hook versus hook with identical body, founder narrative versus case-study framing with the same application page, short versus long application where qualification questions remain constant, or reminder sequence versus reminder sequence with show rate as the outcome. Do not change media, application, calendar, sales script, and offer at the same time. A funnel is a series system, so an unisolated “improvement” makes the data harder to use.

Retargeting should respect intent and frequency. A recent viewer who started an application may need a direct, useful reminder. An older visitor may need a founder explanation, process proof, or a clear answer to an objection. Do not use invented scarcity, pressure, or income claims to force an application. The qualified buyer should understand the next step and be able to decline it.

## Review cadence

Each week, reconcile the KPI chain through closed-won and LTV:CAC, review show rate and response quality, inspect creative fatigue, and select the single constraint with the largest economic leverage. Record policy checks and approved changes in the strategy. Never treat a platform-approved ad as proof that the underlying claim is safe or true.

Before any scale recommendation, confirm all three conditions: the selected CPA or CAC is inside the approved ceiling, quality remains stable through show and close, and the sales or delivery team can absorb the added demand. If only platform CPL improved, the answer is hold and trace the chain. High-ticket campaigns fail most expensively when a seemingly cheap lead volume overwhelms a weak handoff.
